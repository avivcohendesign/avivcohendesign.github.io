MASHKIT.CODE
MASHKIT.CODE is a contemporary fashion house developed from the archive of Maskit.

Working with historical garments, textiles, imagery and material culture, the project extends the visual language of Maskit into the present through new collections, digital objects and contemporary forms of production.

Founded in 1954 by Ruth Dayan, Maskit emerged from a vision to create opportunity through craft. Bringing together textile traditions, artisanal techniques and cultural influences carried by immigrant communities from across the world, the house developed a unique design language rooted in the identity, landscape and material culture of Israel.

It would go on to become Israel's first internationally recognized fashion house.

ORIGIN  //  Maskit archive, 1954 /  Hooded Cape & Skirt Ensemble by Fini Leitersdorf.
Origin
The original item was woven in 1954 in a workshop on Rehov HaSoreg, Jerusalem, on commission for the first Maskit collection. Fini Leitersdorf — the house's founding designer — built it around a single bolt of Yemenite-embroidered silk produced by a family of recent immigrants whose names were never recorded in the workshop's ledger. Its construction took nine weeks. The bodice was hand-fitted; the skirt panels were assembled flat on the floor of the atelier and joined with the irregular hand-stitch tension that distinguished early Maskit pieces from the European couture they were quietly answering. The gown was first shown at the Habima Theatre in 1955. It was photographed by Paul Goldman; the negative is held at the Israel Museum's costume archive.
DESCENDANT  //  Hooded Cape & Skirt Ensemble, 2030 / Edition 03 of 50.
Translation
To bring this piece into the cipher form, the original garment was scanned, its weave structure photographed at thread-level resolution, and its silhouette decomposed into a parametric model that preserves silhouette, drape, and weave behavior under simulated gravity. What was not preserved was perfection. The irregular slub of the original silk, the hand-tension variance across the embroidered yoke, the slight asymmetry of the right cuff — these are not flaws corrected by the rendering. They are signatures. The cipher carries them forward at sixteen-thousand-pixel fidelity. This is not a copy. It is a continuation.
